Fracture Energy Analysis of 3D Printed PLA

How well does 3D-printed PLA actually hold up as a structural material?

Timeline: March 2024 – May 2024 Course: ENGS 24, Materials Science, Dartmouth Tools: Charpy impact tester, Scanning Electron Microscope
  • Materials Science
  • Testing
  • SEM

Overview

A materials science capstone investigating whether 3D-printed PLA is a viable structural material, and specifically how it fails. PLA parts are everywhere in prototyping, but their fracture behavior — especially the ductile-to-brittle transition across temperature — isn't always well understood by the people printing them.

Approach

I used ASTM-standard Charpy impact testing to measure fracture energy and characterize the ductile-brittle transition of PLA across a range of temperatures. To understand why the material failed the way it did, I used Scanning Electron Microscopy to image the fracture surfaces and identify the failure mode at each condition.

Gallery

Results

The testing mapped how PLA's fracture energy and failure mode shift with temperature, showing a clear ductile-brittle transition and distinct fracture surface signatures at each regime under SEM.

Hextronics Internship

Mechanical Engineering Intern — Summer 2026

Hextronics is a small Series-A startup building autonomous "drone-in-a-box" systems: infrastructure that enables industrial inspection drones to launch, fly a route, and land themselves with no one on-site. I spent the summer as Hextronics' mechanical engineering intern, and given the lean team, I got to touch almost every physical part of our product: fabrication, product painting/finishing, and a couple of systems that I designed ground-up. The summer culminated with a public product launch at Commercial UAV (CUAV) Expo 2026 in Las Vegas. Below are three examples of projects that I led at Hextronics.

Drone Arm Lighting System

Industrial inspection drones flying at night or in low visibility need to see and be seen. I was tasked with designing the positional lighting system for the drone's arms. Working with our electrical engineering department, I specced and designed a simple PCB with three high-powered, programmable LEDs mounted on it. We chose the LEDs by reference to federal and international visibility requirements. I designed the board to work in combination with a snap-fit lens that I designed to hold the PCB snugly in place against a surface on the drone arm without the need for adhesives or additional fasteners. I used beam-deflection calculations to ensure that the geometry of the snap doesn't push the plastic beyond its allowable strain limit during installation.

I then took the existing drone arm geometry and hollowed out space for the LED assembly as well as intentional, organized cable routing. I took care to maintain a stiff arm geometry given that the arm's stiffness is critical for vibration mitigation and flight performance.

Drone Docking Station Fabrication

I led fabrication of the drone docking station prototypes for the product's public debut, printing the enclosures on an industrial, 1x1x1 meter print volume 3D printer. I owned the process of tuning, troubleshooting, and operating the printer. Since I was fabricating the actual units that we would show customers and investors at CUAV, I was not satisfied with the results of high FDM print quality in ASA alone. I finished the units using automotive painting techniques (filling, sanding, priming, painting, and clear-coating) to get a surface finish that looked like an injection-molded or thermoformed production part instead of a prototype.

Weather Station

Since our system is designed to operate unattended outdoors, it needs wind and precipitation data prior to flight to ensure safe flying conditions. I designed a weather station around a custom-built Hall-effect anemometer for wind speed, a humidity sensor for precipitation data, and a camera for optional real-time human remote monitoring. These systems feed live readings into the flight management system so it can make flight decisions autonomously.
